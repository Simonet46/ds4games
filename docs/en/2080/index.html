How to play

  1. 1

    Setup

    Lay out a grid of 4 rows by 6 columns: characters, situations, places and inventions.

  2. 2

    The secret code

    Behind the book, the Author rolls the dice. The color is the row (blue character, red situation, yellow place, green invention) and the number is the column.

  3. 3

    Clues without words

    The Author places one Author card next to each row, all four at once.

  4. 4

    Everyone says their code

    Each Reader writes down a code and everyone says it out loud at the same time. The others’ codes stay in view.

  5. 5

    The answer is secret

    The Author tells each Reader, in private, how many digits they got right. Nobody knows the others’ scores.

  6. 6

    End of the game

    Just like Les Secrets de la Tour Eiffel: up to four rounds and a last chance. The Reader who cracks the code wins with the Author.

The rules that make it special

The new rule

In 1812 and the Eiffel Tower game, everyone knows how many each player got right. In 2080 the Author’s answer is private: the others’ codes can help you… or throw you off.

Everything else stays

It plays like Les Secrets de la Tour Eiffel. If you know a Secret Stories game, you already know how to play 2080.

The Reader cards

What 2080 looks like

The 146 chosen cards: 51 characters, 36 situations, 28 places and 31 inventions. They’re the same size as the 1812 and Les Secrets de la Tour Eiffel cards, so you can mix them. Each game, six cards from each row are laid face up.

The Vision cards

113 dream cards

These are the Author cards of 2080. None of them shows the card to guess: the Author looks for a detail that leads to it, and the Readers have to guess what the Author had in mind.

Play a game as a Reader

Every game deals a different table. The Author has rolled the dice and left a Vision card next to each row. Pick one card per row and say your code. Lucy and Max say theirs too: you see their codes, but only you know how many digits you got right.

Who was it?Characters

What the Author was thinking

  1. The jar of fireflies looks like a traffic light and a helicopter lands on the lid: the air taxi pilot.
  2. A merry-go-round flying through the sky with little yellow cars, like taxis.
  3. Someone flying a machine with a controller: piloting.
  4. A cabin hanging in the sky among floating islands: the pilot’s workplace.
In what situation?Situations

What the Author was thinking

  1. A toast between a crystal glass and one made of light: the wedding with an AI.
  2. A table for two with a candle and balloons: a celebration.
  3. A couple kissing in a shower of petals.
  4. Robots and a girl surrounded by smiley faces: love between people and machines.
Where?Places

What the Author was thinking

  1. A fish with a city inside it: Ocean City, the city under the sea.
  2. A living room on the seabed: living underwater.
  3. A fish tank inside a building: the sea inside the city.
  4. A woman with a window onto the sea in her chest, like the dome of Ocean City.
With which invention?Inventions

What the Author was thinking

  1. An umbrella with its own storm inside: the climate window, which brings the weather you choose.
  2. A fridge that opens onto a sunset: a door that shows another climate.
  3. A drone making it rain on a single flowerpot: weather on demand.
  4. A tree with all four seasons at once: choosing the weather.
