The Secrets of the Eiffel Tower

When the Eiffel Tower opened, the whole world wondered where the idea came from. The author of a lost book was the only one who knew the truth. Who was Eiffel with? In what situation? Where? And what were they eating?

Four colored dice secretly pick a character, a situation, a place and a Parisian dish.

Les Secrets de la Tour Eiffel

How to play

  1. 1

    Setup

    Build a grid of 4 rows by 6 columns: characters, situations, places and dishes. The last person to eat a croissant is the Author.

  2. 2

    The secret code

    Behind the book, the Author rolls the dice. The color gives the row (blue character, red situation, yellow place, green dish) and the number gives the column.

  3. 3

    Clues without words

    The Author draws seven Author cards, chooses four and places one next to each row, all at once.

  4. 4

    Every Reader writes

    In secret, each Reader writes the code they believe is right and hands the sheet to the Author.

  5. 5

    How many, not which

    The Author says out loud how many each Reader got right. If nobody cracked it, four new cards go down next to the previous ones.

  6. 6

    End of the game

    There are up to four rounds. The Reader who cracks the code wins together with the Author. If nobody does, there’s one last chance with no new cards: whoever gets it then wins alone.

The rules that make it special

Sign the box

The winner writes their name and the Author’s inside the box, and the Author tells the true story of Gustave Eiffel.

Ties

The player with the most correct answers over the game wins. If it’s still tied, the victory is shared.

Co-op mode

Everyone on the same team, with one score sheet and four turns to crack the code. From 2 to 10 players: the more of you, the more you talk.

The Reader cards

48 cards from Eiffel’s Paris

Twelve cards per row: characters, situations, places and dishes of Paris. Each game puts six of each face up.

The Author cards

60 surreal cards

None of them shows Paris. The Author looks for a detail that points to the right card, and the Readers have to guess what the Author had in mind.

Play a game as a Reader

Every game deals a different table. The Author has already rolled the dice and placed a card next to each row. Pick one card per row and say the code: the Author tells you how many digits are right, but not which ones.

Who was he with?Characters

What the Author was thinking

  1. The boy in the brown cap and light shirt looks like the shoeshine boy.
  2. Another kid who earns a living on the street, climbing out of the river.
  3. A gentleman with a top hat and suitcase: the customer who gets his shoes shined before a trip.
  4. The shiny red boots resting on the skull, like a foot on the shoeshine box.
In what situation?Situations

What the Author was thinking

  1. The black cat points to Le Chat Noir, the most famous cabaret in Montmartre.
  2. An ant riding a rat with a dangling cheese: a crazy act, like a cabaret show.
  3. A man in a suit, drinks flying and the room spinning: the night at the cabaret ended late.
  4. A flashy cape, hat and boots, like a stage costume.
Where?Places

What the Author was thinking

  1. The gargoyles and the beam of light lead to Notre-Dame.
  2. A hooded figure leaping across the rooftops, like the hunchback of Notre-Dame.
  3. The river and the paper boat: Notre-Dame stands on an island in the Seine.
  4. A wrought-iron gate and a huge lock, like the cathedral doors.
What did they eat?Dishes

What the Author was thinking

  1. The flying bottle and glass lead to cognac.
  2. A huge bottle with a wooden stopper.
  3. Cheese, fruit and bread: the end of the meal, when the cognac and cigar come out.
  4. A warm yellow light, like the candle on the table.
